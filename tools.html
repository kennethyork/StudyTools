Read the Bible

The whole Bible in this site's own reader — seven public-domain translations: the World English Bible (Updated), the modernized King James Version and the modernized Revised Version, each with the Apocrypha; the modernized American Standard Version and Young's Literal Translation; the JPS Tanakh of 1917, modernized, for the Old Testament; and the modernized Douay-Rheims, which keeps the Vulgate's numbering and is read on its own, labelled at every chapter. Switch translations as you read, and tap any verse to see it in every translation that carries it, with the cross-references, the public-domain commentary on it, the Greek or Hebrew behind it, where the Prayer Book reads it — and, if your machine can run it, a small language model to ask about the verse, on your own device with nothing sent anywhere.

Start reading →

Search the Bible

Search the whole Bible — Apocrypha included — in the translations this site carries. Every word you type has to be in the verse, so shepherd want finds the verses with both. Results open in the reader or in Study a Passage, and the index is a file on your own machine: nothing you type leaves it.

Search the text →

Verse Notes

Note your Bible as you read: a note on a verse, a note on the chapter under it, a note on a book — and highlight a verse in one of four colours. Tap any verse to write, or use the box beneath the chapter — your annotations on that chapter's verses are listed back beside the text. Everything gathers in one place, searchable by book or by text, and exports as Markdown or JSON. Stored in your browser; nothing sent anywhere.

Study a Passage

Type a reference and get everything this site holds on it in one place: the text in three translations, the cross-references the church has drawn to it, the ground it shares with the Tanakh, the Qur'an and the Book of Mormon, and the original text word by word — Greek for the New Testament, Hebrew for the Old. Printable, so it works as a teaching handout. Built for preparing to teach.

Witness

A cited term base for interfaith translation, on its own site: every card records how each tradition renders a concept and what each rendering loses, with a parity tracker for multi-language statements. No claim without a witness. Code AGPL-3.0, data CC BY-SA 4.0.
