Ask the site

Ask about a verse, a word, a person or a topic. This does not use a language model: it searches what this site already holds — the public-domain commentary, the dictionaries, the cross-references, the topical Bibles, the Greek and Hebrew interlinear and the concordance — and shows you what they say, with each part cited to the book it came from. Nothing is written for you, nothing is downloaded, and nothing leaves the page.

What it can and cannot do

It can find a passage and show you the text in this site's translations, what the commentators said on it, the cross-references the church has drawn to it, the Hebrew or Greek behind it, and where the Prayer Book reads it. It can look a word, person or place up in the dictionaries and the topical Bibles, and it can list every verse a Strong's number appears in.

It cannot reason, summarise, or answer a question that is not in those files — and it will say so rather than writing something plausible. If you want a conversation, the reader offers a small model that runs on your device, which needs WebGPU: check whether this machine can run it.

Every answer on this page is read out of files this site already holds, and each block names the work it came from: commentary from Jamieson, Fausset & Brown (1871), John Calvin (1550s), F. B. Meyer and Haydock (1859); dictionaries from Easton (1897), Smith (1863) and Hastings (1909); the topical Bibles of Nave (1897) and Torrey (1897); cross-references from openbible.info (CC BY 4.0); the Greek from OpenGNT (CC BY-SA 4.0) and the Hebrew from the Open Scriptures Hebrew Bible with STEPBible glosses (CC BY 4.0); the concordance from that same interlinear; and the readings from the Book of Common Prayer (1928), public domain. Nothing is generated, and nothing you type leaves the page.
