Places, their types and the verses they appear in are from the OpenBible.info Bible Geocoding dataset (CC BY 4.0; its own sources number in the hundreds, and some identifications are contested). Click anywhere on the map for the places near that point, pick a journey to follow it, or pick a book and chapter to see only the places that chapter names.

The ground the places stand on is Natural Earth's 1:50m shaded relief and water — hypsometric tints with hill shading, the look of the physical maps printed in the back of a Bible — cropped to these lands and reduced to one JPEG that ships with this site, so there are still no tiles and nothing is fetched while you read. The coastlines, lakes, rivers, today's borders and the modern cities are Natural Earth 1:50m too, all public domain. The map remains a plain equirectangular plot of those coordinates rather than a historical atlas: the terrain is the terrain as it is now, and the borders you can turn on are today's, there only to help you find your way. No dataset in this project holds the political lines of any biblical period, so none are drawn. Zoomed in past what the raster can resolve, the ground fades rather than switching off, so the coastline, rivers and lakes drawn under it stay readable and there is never a blank white plain. A place's dot is as large as how often the Bible names it, and the bar at the foot of the map is kilometres at the middle of the view. The names of the seas and the regions, which no dataset here carries, are this site's own, placed by hand.
