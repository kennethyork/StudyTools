Bible Dictionary

Five public-domain dictionaries together: Easton's (1897), Smith's (1863) and Hastings' (1909) for the people, places and subjects of the Bible; Hitchcock's Bible Names (1869) for what a name means; and Webster's 1828 Dictionary, abridged to the words the King James Version uses — which is where a word like lasciviousness, or the King James sense of conversation, is defined. Where those five leave a gap a reader meets often, there is a short note written for this site, quoting the verse it comes from. A word the King James uses in an older form — aboundeth, pouredst, oxen — is found under its headword, and the entry it belongs to is shown as that word's. Every Scripture citation links to the verse in the Verse Matrix.

Easton's (1897), Smith's (1863), Hastings' (1909) and Hitchcock's Bible Names (1869) come from the NEUU bible-dictionary-dataset (CC BY 4.0), which parses those public-domain works from CCEL's ThML. Webster's 1828 Dictionary is public domain (Noah Webster died in 1843); the text used here is a transcription of it, and this project abridges it to the headwords the King James Version uses — the vocabulary taken from the KJV itself (eBible.org, public domain) — because the complete 1828 runs to 63,000 entries about things no Bible reader looks up — and where its transcription has a failure, or the word is a King James form, compound or spelling that no dictionary here carries, the note is this site's own writing and cites the verse it is checked against.
